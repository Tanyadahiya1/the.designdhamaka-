Why most business websites fail

Most business websites look fine but convert poorly. The reason is almost always the same — they were built to look good, not to drive action. Here are 5 things we check on every website we build.

The logo confusion

When we ask clients "do you have a brand?" they almost always say "yes, we have a logo." A logo is a symbol. Your brand is the entire emotional experience someone has with your business.

What actually makes a brand

  • Your tone of voice — are you fun, professional, bold, warm?
  • Your visual consistency — same fonts, colors, style across everything
  • Your promise — what you reliably deliver, every single time
  • Your story — why you exist, what you stand for
  • Your customer experience — from DM to delivery

Why this matters for small businesses

A business with a consistent brand charges more, gets more referrals, and builds loyalty faster. Apple charges 3x more than competitors not because their phones are 3x better — but because their brand makes people feel something.

How to start building your brand

Start with your "why" — Simon Sinek style. Then define your 3 brand values. Pick a visual identity (logo + colors + fonts) that reflects those values. Apply it consistently across every touchpoint. That is branding.

Stop tracking followers and likes

Follower count and likes feel good but they do not pay bills. Here are the 7 metrics we track for every client — the ones that actually tell you if your marketing is working.

1. Cost Per Lead (CPL)

How much are you paying to get one enquiry? If you spend ₹10,000 on ads and get 20 leads, your CPL is ₹500. Know this number cold.

2. Lead-to-Customer Conversion Rate

Of all enquiries, how many become paying customers? Industry average for service businesses is 20–30%. If you are below 10%, your sales process needs work — not more leads.

3. Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)

Total marketing spend divided by new customers. If you spend ₹50,000 and get 10 customers, CAC = ₹5,000. Your CAC should always be less than your customer lifetime value.

4. Return on Ad Spend (ROAS)

Revenue generated per rupee spent on ads. Minimum viable ROAS is 2x. Good is 4x+. Calculate this monthly for every campaign.

5. Email / WhatsApp open rate

For WhatsApp broadcasts, open rates above 60% are good. Below 30% means your messaging needs work or your list is cold.

6. Website bounce rate

What percentage of visitors leave without clicking anything? Above 70% means your landing page is not matching search intent. Below 40% is excellent.

7. Repeat customer rate

For product businesses: what % of customers buy again? Repeat customers cost 5x less to sell to. If this number is low, invest in retention before acquisition.
